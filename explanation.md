 # Technical Explanation & Timeline

This document explains my involvement with NineSMP from a technical perspective.

It is primarily written for developers, server owners, and anyone interested in understanding what actually happened behind the scenes.

This is my account of the events. Readers should evaluate the information and form their own conclusions.

---

# How It Started

I first met the owner while we were both playing on another Minecraft server.

At that time he was looking for creators for a project called **Bharat SMP**.

During our conversations I suggested that instead of depending entirely on content creators, we could build an independent Minecraft server.

That idea eventually became **NineSMP**.

---

# Initial Development

When development started, the technical side of the project was almost entirely handled by me.

This included:

- Server setup
- Backend infrastructure
- Plugin configuration
- Development workflow
- Deployment
- Networking
- Performance optimization
- Software planning
- Technical guidance

At that point the owner had no experience with Minecraft server development.

Over time he gradually learned how plugin configurations worked and became more involved with server management.

---

# Project Growth

The server continued growing over multiple seasons.

Like every production Minecraft server, it faced problems including:

- Plugin compatibility
- Infrastructure failures
- VPS issues
- DDoS attacks
- Economy balancing
- Configuration bugs
- Performance issues

These are common challenges while operating public Minecraft servers.

---

# Mistakes

One major mistake happened when a world was accidentally deleted.

Unfortunately there was no proper backup available.

That mistake was mine, and I accept responsibility for it.

Apart from that incident, not every technical issue throughout the project's lifetime was caused by me.

Running a production server involves many moving parts and many people.

---

# Development Work

During my time on NineSMP I spent countless hours building and maintaining the server.

Some examples include:

- Infrastructure setup
- Backend systems
- Configuration management
- Automation
- Custom software
- Technical support
- Optimization
- Bug fixing
- Development planning

A large amount of work happened behind the scenes and wasn't visible to players.

---

# Revenue

Near the end of the project, revenue generated by the server was divided equally between both partners.

Based on our agreement, I received my share.

This was not hidden or taken without discussion.

---

# Why I Left

I didn't leave NineSMP because I lost interest in the project.

I left because, over time, I felt that my work and contributions were no longer being respected.

For a long time I worked on the server without expecting a salary. I invested my time into development, infrastructure, backend systems, maintenance, and solving technical issues because I wanted the project to succeed.

Despite this, I felt that my efforts were often overlooked.

One thing that affected me was how ownership was presented publicly.

Internally, we operated as 50–50 partners. However, in public conversations and jokes, I was often introduced as if I was only a developer while the owner took primary credit for the project. Sometimes it was said that ownership was "50% me, 20–30% Neon, and the rest staff." While these comments may have started as jokes, hearing them repeatedly over time made me feel that my actual role was not being acknowledged.

I also felt that the amount of technical work involved in running the server was underestimated.

Running a production Minecraft network involves much more than editing plugin configuration files. It includes infrastructure planning, debugging, deployments, networking, performance optimization, backend systems, maintenance, and solving unexpected production issues.

As time went on, I was increasingly told things like "I made Neon a developer" or "I taught Neon everything." These statements were usually followed by "I was just joking," but after hearing similar remarks repeatedly, I no longer viewed them as harmless jokes.

Over time I also felt that our goals for the project were no longer aligned. From my perspective, continuing the partnership was no longer healthy, so I decided to leave.

---

# After Leaving

After I left the project, public statements about me began appearing in different communities.

I felt that my reputation was being damaged beyond the NineSMP community.

Because of that, I reacted emotionally at times. One of those actions was removing approximately 300 members from the Discord server before leaving.

Looking back, I acknowledge that this was a reaction made during a highly emotional situation.

It does not change my position regarding the disagreements that led to my departure, but I believe it is important to be transparent about what happened.

---

# Public Allegations

After my departure, public statements were made about me.

Among other things, I was accused of:

- Scamming
- Taking server files
- Damaging the project
- Being responsible for the server's failure

I strongly disagree with those allegations.

Rather than responding through arguments or livestreams, I created this repository so people can understand my perspective.

---

# Why I Didn't Respond Immediately

Many people asked why I stayed silent.

The answer is simple.

Arguments on social media rarely solve technical disagreements.

Instead of participating in public drama, I preferred documenting everything in one place.

---

# About Development

Many people see a Minecraft server and assume changing a few configuration files is development.

Professional server development involves much more:

- Designing infrastructure
- Maintaining stability
- Solving production failures
- Managing deployments
- Debugging difficult issues
- Planning architecture
- Maintaining backend systems

Those responsibilities often remain invisible to players.
